# Third-Party Attributions

This skill synthesizes established UX writing and content design practice. The procedure,
phrasing, tables, and examples in this skill are original work licensed under MIT (see
`LICENSE`). The underlying frameworks and the practitioners who named them are credited below.

## Frameworks referenced

**Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g)** — the *3 C's of informational microcopy* (Clear, Concise,
Character) and the *3 I's of microcopy* (Inform, Influence, Interact), plus NN/g's published
guidance on writing digital copy for domain experts, which informs the register-by-expertise
section of `references/voice-and-tone.md`. The category names are NN/g's; the rules, tables,
and examples built on them here are original.

**Torrey Podmajersky** — *Strategic Writing for UX* (O'Reilly, 2019). The stance that product
copy is a strategic instrument tied to business goals and measurable outcomes, and the practice
of defining a voice through explicit, checkable principles, informs the brief and voice sections.

**Yael Ben-David** — *The Fundamentals of UX Writing* (2023). Informs the treatment of voice
versus tone as separate, independently specified layers.

**Nicole Fenton & Kate Kiefer Lee** — *Nicely Said: Writing for the Web with Authority and
Empathy* (New Riders, 2014). Informs the tone-mapping approach — matching register to the
reader's emotional state rather than to the screen's importance.

**Harry Brignull** — the *dark patterns* taxonomy (deceptive.design). The pattern names used in
`references/antipatterns-and-dark-patterns.md` (confirmshaming, roach motel, trick wording,
forced continuity, nagging, disguised ads, hidden costs) originate in that public taxonomy. The
"honest version" column, the severity ladder, and the audit procedure are original.

**Jakob Nielsen** — usability heuristics, in particular *Help users recognize, diagnose, and
recover from errors*, which underlies the what / why / what-next contract in
`references/error-message-anatomy.md`.

**The "This, but not that" voice method** — established practice in content strategy, popularized
by public brand voice guides. Mailchimp's *Content Style Guide* (voice and tone section, published
openly under CC BY-NC 4.0) is the most cited example, and three of its pairs — *fun but not
childish, confident but not cocky, expert but not bossy* — are quoted in
`references/voice-chart.md` as illustrations of the method, with attribution, under fair use as
short factual excerpts. All other pairs, the bull's-eye framing, the synonym-trap test, and the
chart structure in that file are original.

**Voice Chart structure** — the practice of tabulating voice as principles × aspects (concepts,
vocabulary, verbosity, grammar) is standard content-strategy method, described in several public
UX writing curricula. The specific rows, cells, banned-word lists, and worked example are original.

**"Adjective latching" and few-shot prompting for tone** — the observation that language models
over-index on a single tone adjective, and that paired good/bad examples outperform descriptive
instructions, comes from public practitioner writing on prompting for content design. The
pastiche-tell taxonomy in `references/golden-standards.md` (antithesis, escalating triples,
em-dash reveals, advertising verbs, and the rest) is original analysis.

## Regulatory references

Mentions of the EU Digital Services Act, GDPR consent requirements, the Unfair Commercial
Practices Directive, and US FTC guidance on negative option marketing are pointers to public law
and regulator guidance. They are context for why certain patterns are treated as defects. **They
are not legal advice** — consult counsel for any specific compliance question.

## What this skill is NOT derived from

- No text was copied from any book, article, blog post, or course. All tables, rules, and
  examples are original phrasing.
- No proprietary content style guide (Mailchimp, Shopify Polaris, IBM Carbon, Atlassian, GOV.UK,
  or any vendor design system) is reproduced or closely paraphrased. Those exist publicly as
  examples of the practice; this skill stands on its own.
- All example copy, product names, and glossary entries are invented for illustration and do not
  reproduce any real product's strings.

## How to attribute when you remix

If you fork this skill or build a derivative, please keep:

1. This `ATTRIBUTION.md`
2. The MIT `LICENSE`
3. A visible credit to NN/g for the 3 C's / 3 I's naming and to Harry Brignull for the dark
   patterns taxonomy

That keeps the chain of credit intact without burdening downstream users.
