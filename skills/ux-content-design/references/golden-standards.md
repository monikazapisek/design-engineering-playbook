# Golden Standards

Load this **before drafting anything**, not after. Examples do more to fix tone than any
description of tone. If you read only one reference, read this one.

---

## Part 1 — The pastiche tells

These are the specific structural habits that make copy sound machine-written. They are not
matters of taste. Each one is a recognizable rhetorical device borrowed from advertising, and
each one signals "this was written to be admired" rather than "this was written to be used."

**If you catch yourself producing any of these, delete the sentence and state the fact instead.**

| Tell | Example | Why it fails | Instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Antithesis** | *"You don't have a writing problem. You have a states problem."* | Nobody talks like this. It's a slogan structure — it announces cleverness before information. | Say the one true thing: *"The empty state and the error state are usually the same string. They shouldn't be."* |
| **"Not just X, it's Y"** | *"It's not just a form — it's a conversation."* | Inflates an ordinary thing by denying it's ordinary. | It's a form. Say what the form does. |
| **Rule of three, escalating** | *"Faster, simpler, and finally under control."* | Rhythm doing the work of content. The third item is always the vaguest. | One item, specific. |
| **Fragment for emphasis** | *"Every state. Written. Before handoff."* | Punctuation as drama. Reads as a voiceover. | One complete sentence. |
| **Rhetorical question opener** | *"Ever wondered why your copy keeps bouncing?"* | Fake dialogue. The reader didn't ask. | State the situation directly. |
| **Em-dash reveal** | *"There's one thing that breaks every handoff — the states nobody wrote."* | Manufactured suspense in a sentence that has none. | Put the point first. |
| **"Here's the thing" / "The result?"** | *"The result? Copy that ships."* | Talk-show connective tissue. | Delete it; the next sentence works alone. |
| **Abstract noun stack** | *"Clarity, confidence, and control."* | Three words, no referent. Unfalsifiable and unmemorable. | Name the concrete thing that changes. |
| **Superlative + vague noun** | *"Best-in-class content tooling"* | Nothing checkable. | *"Checks 12 states per screen"* — or say nothing. |
| **Verbed brand energy** | *"Unlock, elevate, supercharge, transform"* | Advertising verbs. Nobody unlocks anything. | *Turn on. Improve. Speed up. Change.* |
| **False intimacy** | *"We've all been there."* | Assumes a shared experience to manufacture rapport. | Describe the situation; let recognition happen or not. |
| **Manufactured stakes** | *"Before it costs you a customer."* | Fear as a motivator, usually unearned. | State the actual consequence, or drop it. |

### The read-aloud gate

Before shipping any string, read it aloud and ask one question:

> **Would a person say this to another person sitting next to them?**

Not "is it grammatical", not "is it on brand". Would a human say it, out loud, to someone they
work with. If the honest answer is "only in an ad" or "only on a slide", rewrite it.

This single check catches more pastiche than every rule above combined, because the rules describe
symptoms and this one describes the disease.

## Part 2 — Golden pairs

Three pairs per category. Study the shape, not the words.

### Errors

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Oops! Something went wrong on our end. We're working hard to fix it!"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"We couldn't save your changes. They're still here — try again."* |
| Why | The bad one performs regret and says nothing. The good one says what failed, what survived, what to do. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Authorization failure. Invalid credentials supplied."* |
| ✅ Natural | *"That email and password don't match. Try again, or reset your password."* |
| Why | The bad one is the system talking to itself. The good one is a person telling you what to do. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Payment unsuccessful. Please contact your financial institution for further assistance."* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Your bank declined the payment. You haven't been charged. Try another card, or call your bank and try again."* |
| Why | The bad one hands you off. The good one answers the only question that matters — did my money move — then gives two ways forward. |

### Empty states

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Your journey starts here! Create your first project and unlock the power of collaboration."* |
| ✅ Natural | *"No projects yet. Create one to start tracking work with your team."* |
| Why | Nobody's journey starts in a table view. The good one says what this screen is and what to do. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Nothing to see here… yet!"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"No results for “acme corp”. Try a shorter search, or clear the date filter."* |
| Why | The bad one is a joke where help should be. The good one shows the query back — which is how people find their own typo. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"All caught up! You're crushing it. 🎉"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Nothing left to review."* |
| Why | Finishing a queue is not an achievement. Say it and get out of the way. |

### Onboarding and value

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Our revolutionary search engine transforms how you find information."* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Search across every document, including scanned PDFs."* |
| Why | The bad one describes the company's opinion of itself. The good one describes what you can now do. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Take control of your finances with powerful, intuitive tools designed for you."* |
| ✅ Natural | *"See what you're owed, and who's late paying."* |
| Why | Four adjectives, zero information vs. two concrete things you can now see. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Let's get you set up for success! It'll only take a minute. 🚀"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Three steps: add your company details, connect a bank account, send your first invoice."* |
| Why | The bad one is enthusiasm about the setup. The good one is the setup. |

### Buttons

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Payment"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Place order"* |
| Why | A noun tells you the topic. A verb tells you what happens when you press it. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Let's go!"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Claim offer"* |
| Why | The bad one works only next to the heading. The good one survives being read alone by a screen reader. |

### Login and returning users

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Hi there! Let's do this thinggg 🙌"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Welcome back. Log in and we'll pick up where you left off."* |
| Why | Chumminess is not warmth. The good one is friendly and still tells you what happens. |

### Sensitive facts — money, health, eligibility

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Congrats! You're eligible for $7,000! 🎉"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"You're eligible for up to $7,000."* |
| Why | The bad one decides how the reader feels about a loan. *Up to* is also the honest quantifier. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Payment method expired"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Update your card to keep your subscription active. The one ending 1234 expired."* |
| Why | The bad one states a condition. The good one states what to do about it, then the fact. |

### Confirmations and destructive actions

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Are you sure? This action cannot be undone!"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Delete “Q3 report”? This also removes its shared links. It stays in trash for 30 days."* |
| Why | The bad one raises alarm without information. The good one lets you decide. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"We're sad to see you go! 😢 Are you sure you want to cancel?"* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Your plan stays active until 14 March. After that, exports become read-only and your team loses access."* |
| Why | The bad one makes it about the company's feelings. The good one tells you what you're deciding. |

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ Pastiche | *"Success! Your changes have been saved successfully."* |
| ✅ Natural | *"Saved."* |
| Why | Saving is not news. One word, then get out of the way. |

## Part 3 — Product copy is not marketing copy

The single most useful distinction, and the one that prevents most pastiche.

| | Marketing | Product (this skill) |
|---|---|---|
| **Reader** | Hasn't bought, hasn't signed up | Already inside, mid-task |
| **Job** | Attract, persuade, differentiate | Help them finish, reduce uncertainty |
| **Success** | They click, they sign up | They complete the task and don't think about the words |
| **Register** | Memorable, emotive, figurative | Utilitarian, plain, transparent |
| **Adjectives** | Load-bearing | Almost always removable |
| **Good means** | Captivating | Solid and functional |

**The instruction:** you are not selling anything. Someone is standing in front of a screen with a
task. Your job is to get them through it with the fewest words that leave them in control.

This holds even when the copy has to help a decision — a plan comparison, an upgrade prompt, a
feature announcement. Inside the product, persuasion means **giving people what they need to
choose correctly**, not making the option sound exciting. State what it does, what it costs, and
what happens if they don't. Someone who upgrades because a sentence was clever churns; someone who
upgrades because they understood what they were getting, stays.

**The one legitimate use of marketing register in this skill:** never. If the task genuinely
requires a campaign tagline, say so and route it elsewhere — `do_not_use_for` lists it.

## Part 4 — Writing your own examples into a prompt

When you hand this work to another model, another writer, or a batch job, examples travel better
than rules. Use a fixed structure so the pattern is detectable:

```
Context:   You are a UX writer, not a marketer. You help someone finish a task.
Scenario:  Where the user is and what just happened.
Input:     The raw facts — system state, error code, constraint, character budget.
Output:    The ideal string.
```

Two worked entries, one of each polarity:

```
Scenario:  Subscription will lapse; the card on file expired.
Input:     card_expired, last4=1234, grace_period=7d, max 60 chars
Output:    Update your card to keep your subscription active.

Scenario:  Same.
Avoid:     Oops! Don't let the magic stop — renew your amazing card now!
Why:       Sells instead of helping. "Magic" and "amazing" describe nothing.
            No mention of what actually happens if they ignore it.
```

Pair every good example with the bad one **for the same scenario**. A good example alone teaches
style; the contrast teaches the boundary, and the boundary is what stops the drift.

Three to five pairs is enough. More than that and later pairs stop influencing the output.

## Part 5 — Iterating when the output is still wrong

- **Ask for three to five variants, not one.** A single generation gets stuck on whatever
  adjective it latched onto first. Variants break the fixation, and the fourth is often the
  human one.
- **Test with a frustrated user, not a happy one.** Voice is easy to hold when things go well.
  Run the scenario where the payment failed twice and the user is about to leave — if the copy
  stays plain and useful there, the voice is real.
- **Promote winners into the example set.** When a string is genuinely good, add it to Part 2 as
  a new golden pair. The example set should grow from real work, not stay frozen at whatever the
  first author imagined.
- **Cut, then judge.** Before evaluating a draft, delete every adjective and adverb. Read what
  remains. Usually it is better, and now you know which modifiers to restore.

## Part 6 — Self-check before shipping

- [ ] Read aloud — would a person say this to someone sitting next to them?
- [ ] Zero tells from Part 1
- [ ] Every adjective earns its place — remove each one and check whether meaning is lost
- [ ] Nothing claimed that the product doesn't control
- [ ] No exclamation mark in an error, warning, or confirmation
- [ ] No emoji in a failure state
- [ ] If a sentence would work as a slide title, rewrite it
- [ ] The user could act on this without reading it twice
